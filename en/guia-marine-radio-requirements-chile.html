Guide for sailors in Chile

Radio Requirements in Chile

What radio equipment, certificates and documents Chile requires, separating the IMO rules from what Chile adds.

By Captain Rodrigo Troncoso · Updated November 2026 · 15 min read

This guide gathers the radio equipment, operator certificates and documents that Chilean rules require of vessels, and separates what comes from international conventions from what Chile adds. It covers radiocommunications only: it does not include general safety equipment or navigation procedures. For the overall picture in any country, see the worldwide marine radio guide.

Read it as a planning guide. It is based on official Directemar texts and IMO technical sources. Several documents are years old and may have been amended. Anything marked Confirm must be checked with the relevant Maritime Governorate or Port Captaincy before buying equipment or registering a vessel. This guide describes Chilean vessels: for a foreign sailboat only visiting Chile, the rule that applies is not clear in the texts I reviewed, so ask the Captaincy at the first port.

How to read this guide: three regulatory layers

IMO conventions set a common minimum for signatory countries, and each State may add its own requirements. That is why each requirement carries an origin tag.

TagLayerWhat it means
IMOInternational conventions (SOLAS ch. IV, STCW A-IV/2)Minimum required in all signatory countries. It applies to passenger ships and cargo ships of 300 GT or more on international voyages.
CHILENational regulation (D.S. 392, DGTM circulars, D.S. 214)Chile's own requirements that add to the IMO ones or extend similar rules to vessels the IMO does not cover, such as small and recreational vessels.
LOCALMaritime authority (Governorate, Captaincy)Reasoned resolutions, exemptions and case-by-case inspections. Confirm in writing with the authority.

Sea areas A1 to A4

The area where the vessel sails determines what equipment it must carry. The definitions are the IMO's and Chile adopts them in article 9 of D.S. 392; the Directorate General sets the extent of each area in Chilean waters.

AreaDefinitionBase means
A1Radiotelephone coverage of at least one VHF coast station, with continuous DSC alerting.VHF with DSC (channel 70)
A2Excludes A1. Coverage of at least one MF coast station, with continuous DSC alerting.MF with DSC (2,187.5 kHz)
A3Excludes A1 and A2. Coverage of an INMARSAT geostationary satellite with continuous alerting.Satellite, or MF/HF
A4Everything outside A1, A2 and A3, such as the polar areas.MF/HF

The 2024 SOLAS amendments redefine A3 to include other recognised mobile satellite services, such as Iridium. I have not yet found how Chile incorporates this: confirm with the Directorate General.

Minimum equipment of every vessel

CHILEArticle 8 of D.S. 392 requires this equipment of every vessel, regardless of area, in addition to what the area requires. It applies to domestic navigation too, not only international.

N°EquipmentOrigin
1VHF installation with DSC distress alerts on channel 70 and radiotelephony on at least channels 06, 13 and 16.IMOCHILE
2VHF installation with continuous watch on channel 70 (may be combined with the previous one).IMOCHILE
3Radar transponder in the 9 GHz band (SART).IMOCHILE
4NAVTEX receiver, if the vessel sails in areas with NAVTEX service.IMOCHILE
5Reception of maritime safety information by INMARSAT EGC or HF NBDP, if there is no NAVTEX service.IMOCHILE
6Satellite emergency position-indicating radio beacon (EPIRB).IMOCHILE

Functional capabilities of every vessel at sea (art. 3, D.S. 392): transmit distress alerts by two separate and independent means; receive shore-to-ship distress alerts; ship-to-ship and search-and-rescue communications; on-scene communications; locating signals; maritime safety information; bridge-to-bridge communications; and general communications with shore networks. No vessel may put to sea if it cannot perform the distress and safety functions in numbers 1 to 8 of that article (art. 5).

Passenger ships (additional)

  • IMOCHILEDistress panel at the conning position, with a push button that starts the alert on all required means and shows which were activated (art. 7).
  • IMOCHILEDistress alarm panel with visual and audible indication of alerts received.
  • IMOCHILEAutomatic, continuous position information to all equipment, so it is included in the initial alert.

Backup power, antennas and maintenance

  • CHILEBackup power for the VHF and the area's equipment for 1 hour (if an approved emergency source feeds the radio) or 6 hours (if there is none). Circuits independent from propulsion (arts. 17 to 19).
  • CHILEBatteries: automatic charging within 10 hours, capacity test every 12 months, compartment marked "Radio Batteries" and independent panel (arts. 20 and 21).
  • CHILEVessels under 300 GT in exclusively domestic navigation, and fishing vessels under 24 m, may power the radio directly from batteries (art. 23).
  • IMOCHILEMaintenance: in A1 and A2, one of these methods: equipment duplication, shore-based or at-sea maintenance. In A3 and A4, a combination of at least two (arts. 29 and 30).
  • CHILEService and operation manuals on board, with the operation manual translated into Spanish, plus spares and tools per the manufacturer (art. 32). Installation, repair and maintenance only by a technical entity recognised by the Directorate General (arts. 24 and 27).

Requirements by vessel type and class

Larger vessels on international voyages

  • IMOPassenger ships of any size and cargo ships of 300 GT or more on international voyages: full GMDSS equipment by area, GMDSS operators certified under STCW and a ship station licence.
  • CHILEThey also comply with all of D.S. 392: type-approved equipment, registered and coded EPIRB, documents and radio log.

Vessels in domestic navigation

  • CHILED.S. 392 applies to every vessel, including those not covered by SOLAS (art. 8: "every vessel"). The Director General may partially exempt vessels that sail only in national waters and for which full application is unreasonable given their size, through a reasoned resolution setting conditions (art. 10).
  • LOCALTo know whether a specific vessel is exempt, ask the Maritime Governorate for the resolution.

Small commercial vessels (50 GT or less)

CHILECircular O-71/010, Annex E (change 1, 2000). Confirm it is still in force.

Small vessel typeRadiocommunications equipment and requirements
A. Passenger (any GT) and cargo or fishing 25 to 50 GT
  • VHF transceiver up to 25 W, with independent batteries and charger.
  • Ship Station Licence.
  • One crew member with a Radiotelephone Operator Certificate, restricted category.
  • Plate on the equipment with the call sign.
  • Summary table of distress, urgency and safety procedures.
  • Communications log (may be combined with the sea and port log).
  • Radiotelephone Handbook of the Maritime Mobile Service.
  • Clock of 12.5 cm diameter.
  • Call sign painted on both sides of the bridge or deckhouse, and on top of it.
  • Two-way VHF radiotelephone and radar transponder or reflector (only if there is a liferaft).
B. Cargo or fishing under 25 GT going beyond 2 miles
  • VHF transceiver up to 25 W, with independent batteries and charger.
  • Ship Station Licence.
  • One crew member with a Radiotelephone Operator certificate, restricted category.
  • Call sign painted on both sides of the bridge or deckhouse.
C. River and lake passenger vesselsSame items as group A. If there is no radio station in the area, the owner must install a base station approved by the Maritime Authority.
D. Other requirements
  • Every VHF must have DSC on channel 70 (final deadline: 1 February 2005).
  • An approved-type EPIRB is recommended; vessels within range of a VHF coast station may use a VHF channel 70 EPIRB.
  • All equipment must be DGTM type-approved.
  • Cargo or passenger vessels that only sail within a bay or port may be exempt, if the authority considers it reasonable.
  • Small vessels without VHF must carry at least a portable AM-FM radio receiver.

Point to clarify. Circular O-71/010 says the EPIRB is "recommended", while article 8 of D.S. 392 (from 2001, later) requires it of every vessel. For a small passenger vessel, confirm with the Captaincy which prevails, or whether there is an exemption resolution.

Recreational vessels

CHILECircular A-41/014, Annex A. Recreational vessels over 50 GT are registered as larger vessels, and those of 50 GT or less as small vessels at the Port Captaincy. The classification (offshore, coastal 60 NM, coastal 12 NM, bay) is assigned by the Maritime Authority and recorded on the registration certificate and the seaworthiness certificate (valid for 6 years). More on the procedure in the registration guide and on the rest of the equipment in the safety equipment guide.

Additional rules in the circular:

  • Coastal vessels sailing beyond the range of their DSC VHF must add MF-HF with DSC or satellite communications ensuring a link with coast stations.
  • Coastal vessels may replace the EPIRB with a satellite GPS locator such as SPOT.
  • Power for equipment in offshore and coastal 60 NM classes must be on banks independent of other services, with 6 hours of emergency operation.
  • DSC equipment, EPIRBs or equivalents and AIS must be registered and have an MMSI assigned by the Maritime Authority. DSC must be connected to a GPS signal.
  • Bay vessels with a solo skipper must carry a handheld marine VHF or a mobile phone in a waterproof case.
  • Those with satellite communications must have the telephone numbers of MRCC Chile and the district MRCCs registered.

IMORecreational vessels are not within SOLAS chapter IV unless they are passenger vessels on international voyages. The requirements in this table are a Chilean rule.

Operators and qualifying certificates

CHILEEvery vessel with a radio installation must include in its safety crew personnel with a title or certificate qualified by the Directorate General, and assign one of them primary responsibility for radiocommunications during distress (art. 41). The class and number of radio operators are set by resolution and recorded on the Safe Manning Certificate (art. 44).

IMOSTCW A-IV/2 defines two certificates for SOLAS vessels: GOC, for all areas A1 to A4, and ROC, for A1 only. The LRC and SRC certificates, used recreationally and by non-SOLAS vessels, are not part of STCW and D.S. 392 does not mention them. Chile has its own national categories.

Documents, licences and station identification

CHILEMandatory documents at every ship station (art. 61):

  • Ship Station Licence.
  • Operator certificate(s).
  • Radio log in approved format (kept at least 2 years after completion, arts. 36 to 40).
  • Radio Safety Certificate or General Safety Certificate.
  • Inventory of the approved radio installation.

CHILEAdditional on vessels in domestic maritime navigation:

  • Updated list of stations it can communicate with during its voyage, with schedules and frequencies, and list of stations providing maritime safety information in its area.
  • Summary table of distress, urgency and safety procedures.
  • Manual for use in the Maritime Mobile Service (the TM-021 Radiotelephone Handbook is accepted instead) and the General Radiocommunications Regulation.
  • Operation manuals for all equipment, antenna plan and electrical interconnection diagram with each unit's consumption.

Ship station licence (arts. 56 to 60). Granted by the Directorate General and valid for up to 5 years. It lapses on expiry without renewal, on change of vessel name or owner, change of flag, significant equipment changes and de-registration, among other causes.

Identification. The call sign, the ISMM number (the Chilean MMSI) and other signals go on a plate or are painted in view from the operating position. The call sign and ISMM stay with the vessel while it keeps Chilean registration (art. 54). On pleasure craft and other special-service vessels, the call sign is painted on both sides of the bridge or deckhouse (black on white) and on the roof (black on orange).

Beacons (art. 15). They must be type-approved, registered in the Directorate General's database, coded with the vessel's ISMM and have a certificate from a recognised technical entity attesting to their operability and correct coding.

Approved equipment (art. 14). All equipment used to comply with the regulation must be type-approved by the Directorate General, with an Equipment Approval Certificate. Voluntary GMDSS-compatible equipment must also be type-approved (art. 11).

IMOCHILEWatch. While at sea, vessels with VHF keep a continuous watch on DSC channel 70. With MF, on 2,187.5 kHz; with MF/HF, on the corresponding DSC frequencies; with an INMARSAT earth station, on the shore-to-ship alert signal (art. 45).

If you register a foreign sailboat in Chile

When registered in Chile, the vessel will need a Chilean ship station licence, call sign and ISMM (the call sign and ISMM stay with the vessel while it keeps Chilean registration, art. 54). It is wise to budget for type-approved radio equipment, certification and coding of the beacon, and the licence within the initial investment. Confirm the procedure for foreign vessels with the Captaincy. See the registration guide.

Points to confirm with the maritime authority

  • Whether circular A-41/014 (2015, change 2016) and O-71/010 (2000) have later versions or amendments.
  • Text of the Director General's resolution setting equipment by area (art. 9 of D.S. 392).
  • Official extent of areas A1 and A2 in Chilean waters, and how satellite services other than INMARSAT are treated after the 2024 SOLAS amendments.
  • Whether a small passenger vessel under 50 GT must carry an EPIRB (art. 8) or whether circular O-71/010 only recommends it.
  • Exact requirements of the restricted operator course: authorised entities, age, documents and exam.
  • Current status of the channel 16 watch (D.S. 392 required it until 2005, extendable by the Director General).

Where to ask: Valparaíso Maritime Governorate (Directemar), or the Port Captaincy of the municipality where the vessel is registered or calls.
