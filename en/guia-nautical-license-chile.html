Boating License in Chile: Bay Skipper, Coastal and Offshore Captain

Which licence you need, what the official sources require, and where to double-check before you start.

By Captain Rodrigo Troncoso  ·  Updated October 2026  ·  9 min read

One of the most common questions I get from people planning to sail in Chile — whether they're buying their first boat or arriving from abroad — is how the licensing system works. It is regulated by the General Regulation on Nautical Sports and Recreational Diving, and the official documentation can be heavy going if you're not used to maritime administrative language.

This guide summarises the system in practical terms: which licence you need for what you want to sail, what the official sources require, and how the process works. It is based on the General Regulation on Nautical Sports and Recreational Diving (TM-002, updated April 2022, in Spanish) and on the procedure pages published by Directemar and ChileAtiende. Requirements can change, so always confirm with your Capitanía de Puerto before starting the process.

The licensing system in Chile

The authority that regulates recreational boating and issues licences is Directemar — the General Directorate of the Maritime Territory and Merchant Marine, part of the Chilean Navy. A recreational boating licence is a document granted by the Maritime Authority; a course diploma or a yacht club certificate does not replace it.

The Regulation defines four recreational navigation licences, from lowest to highest:

Level 1
Patrón de Lancha Deportiva de Bahía (Bay Motorboat Skipper)
Motor only · Sheltered waters · From age 14
Command of recreational bay craft powered exclusively by engine, in sheltered waters inside ports, bays, rivers and lakes, up to the limit set by the local Maritime Authority.
Level 2
Patrón Deportivo de Bahía (Bay Skipper)
Sail and motor · Sheltered waters · From age 14
Command of all types of recreational bay craft (sail and motor) in sheltered waters inside ports, bays, rivers and lakes, up to the limit set by the local Maritime Authority. Holders of the motorboat licence only need to pass a practical exam on handling a bay sailing craft.
Level 3
Capitán Deportivo Costero (Coastal Captain)
Up to 60 nm from the coast · Requires Patrón Deportivo de Bahía
Command of all types of coastal and offshore recreational vessels on passages from one point of the coast to another, up to 60 nautical miles from shore. Also covers command of bay craft.
Level 4
Capitán Deportivo de Alta Mar (Offshore Captain)
Any navigation, including ocean passages
Requires the Coastal Captain licence and proof of at least 20 passages (5 of them at night) and 200 hours in command, shown by the vessel's logbook or a certificate from the boating entity. Granted by the Directorate General by resolution.

Which one do I need? It depends on the area and type of navigation. The Bay licences are limited to sheltered waters (ports, bays, rivers and lakes) up to the limit set by the Maritime Authority in your area. For coastal passages between ports, as a cruising sailboat does, the licence that covers it is the Capitán Deportivo Costero (up to 60 nm from shore). The Offshore licence is for ocean navigation. To find out which limit applies in your area, ask at the relevant Capitanía de Puerto.

Official sources do not fully agree. Directemar's procedure page gives 17 as the minimum age for the Coastal Captain licence, while the Regulation (art. 16) says 18. For Offshore, the procedure page mentions holding the Coastal licence for at least one year, while the Regulation also requires 20 passages and 200 hours in command. The procedure page also omits the Motorboat Skipper licence, which does appear in the Regulation (art. 12). Before planning, confirm the current requirement with your Capitanía de Puerto.

General requirements

To obtain any navigation licence, the Regulation (art. 16) and the official procedure pages ask for:

  • Minimum age: 14 for Motorboat Skipper and Bay Skipper; 18 for Coastal Captain (and for Offshore, according to Directemar's page, which gives 17 for Coastal; see the notice above). Minors need notarised authorisation from a parent or guardian.
  • Medical certificate: showing physical fitness compatible with recreational boating. ChileAtiende asks that it carries the specialist's stamp and signature.
  • Training courses and exams for the Motorboat Skipper, Bay Skipper and Coastal Captain licences, as set out in the Regulation.
  • A prior licence where applicable: Bay Skipper to apply for Coastal, and Coastal to apply for Offshore.
  • Documents: application form, a photocopy of the national ID card (both sides) and colour photographs (Directemar's page asks for two 4×4 cm photos and ChileAtiende's for one 3×3 cm photo with name and ID number; confirm the format with your Capitanía). If you belong to the Chilean AFP pension system, Directemar also asks for an AFP certificate stating that you have not retired on disability grounds.

Courses and exams

Training courses are delivered by nautical training entities authorised by the Directorate General, and must follow the programmes it sets. According to the Regulation (art. 17), the subjects include, for example, seamanship and manoeuvring, navigation, safety and first aid, meteorology, maritime regulations and marine engine operation; the Coastal Captain course adds maritime communications, search and rescue, and heavy-weather navigation.

Exams are taken at the Capitanías de Puerto or at authorised training entities (art. 19). To pass the theory exam you need 60% in every subject, and you must also pass the common and emergency manoeuvres on the vessel type for the licence you are applying for. Candidates who sit the exam at a Capitanía must provide the vessel and equipment for the practical exam (art. 23).

The process step by step

  1. 01
    Decide which licence you need
    According to the type of vessel and the area where you will sail. If you are unsure between Bay and Coastal, ask the Capitanía de Puerto which limit applies in your area.
  2. 02
    Choose an authorised training entity
    Confirm it is authorised by the Directorate General and that the course matches the licence you want. Ask which vessel they use for practice and what their pass rate is.
  3. 03
    Gather your documents
    Medical certificate, ID card, photographs and, where applicable, notarised authorisation and AFP certificate. Ask your Capitanía how long a medical certificate is accepted before you submit it.
  4. 04
    Pass the course and the exams
    Theory exam with 60% in every subject, and a practical exam covering common and emergency manoeuvres.
  5. 05
    Submit your application
    At any Capitanía de Puerto in the country or at the Nautical Sports office in Santiago (Av. Portales 4040, inside the Estación Naval Metropolitana, Quinta Normal). Directemar also publishes the application form on its website.
  6. 06
    Receive your licence
    You receive the grant resolution (form DGTM 400) and the Professional Identity Card (TIP). Directemar states a five-working-day turnaround; ChileAtiende gives a maximum of two working days.

Validity and renewal

Recreational boating licences are valid for five years (art. 24). To renew, you submit a medical certificate showing you still meet the physical fitness requirement, with no theory or practical exams. According to ChileAtiende, renewal is requested with the application form, your current licence, a photograph and the medical certificate. It is your responsibility to keep your personal details up to date with the Maritime Authority.

If you are a professional mariner

The Regulation (art. 22) allows line officers of the Chilean Navy, of the Chilean Merchant Marine and of special vessels, including fishing vessels, to obtain the Motorboat Skipper, Bay Skipper or Coastal Captain licences by passing a practical exam only. Navy ratings can do so for the Motorboat and Bay licences, and Merchant Marine and special-vessel crew for the Motorboat licence. Ask your Capitanía how your qualification is documented.

Where to study

Only nautical training entities authorised by the Directorate General may deliver the courses. Their courses must follow the official programmes, and navigation and seamanship instructors must hold a higher licence than the one they teach (at least Coastal Captain). Before enrolling, ask for the resolution that authorises the entity and confirm with your Capitanía that it is current.

Tip: compare at least two entities. Ask which vessel they use for practice, how many sailing hours the course includes and what happens if you fail an exam.

Foreign licences in Chile

The Regulation (art. 11) states that licences issued in other countries or by international associations may be recognised, for Chileans and foreigners, according to their equivalence and the conditions the Director General sets by reasoned resolution. In other words, recognition is not automatic: it depends on those conditions.

In addition, foreign recreational boaters must (art. 36): request written authorisation to sail Chilean inland waters from the Maritime Authority at the first Chilean port of arrival, comply with the rules it sets, and carry charts for the sailing area. To find out whether your licence is recognised and which documents to bring, ask at the Capitanía de Puerto of your first port of arrival.
