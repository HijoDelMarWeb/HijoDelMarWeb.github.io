Nautical License in Chile: Types, Requirements & How to Get One

Which licence you need, how to get it, and what nobody tells you until you're already in the process.

By Captain Rodrigo Troncoso  ·  July 2026  ·  12 min read

One of the most common questions I get from people planning to sail in Chile — whether they're buying their first boat or arriving from abroad — is how the nautical licensing system works. The Chilean system is well-structured but has its quirks, and Directemar's official documentation can be heavy going if you're not used to maritime administrative language.

This guide explains the system in practical terms: which licence you need based on what you want to sail, what the actual process looks like, and what it costs.

Chile's licence framework

In Chile, the authority that regulates recreational navigation and issues skipper licences is Directemar — the General Directorate of Maritime Territory and Merchant Marine, a branch of the Chilean Navy. Directemar sets requirements, administers exams, and issues all titles. There are no "private" licences or club-issued certificates with legal standing to operate in Chilean jurisdictional waters — everything goes through Directemar.

There are four relevant licences for recreational sailing, in ascending order of scope:

Level 1
Patrón de Motonauta
Vessels up to 12 m · Within 12 nm of the coast
For recreational motor and small sailing vessels in inshore and nearshore waters. The entry-level licence. Covers personal use or charter in the restricted zone.
Level 2 · Most popular
Patrón de Yate
Sail and power · Up to 200 nm offshore
The most sought-after licence for cruising sailors. Covers extended coastal and some offshore passages. Standard requirement to obtain a Zarpe (sailing permit) for longer voyages in Chilean waters.
Level 3
Patrón de Nave Nacional (PNN)
Coastal and cabotage navigation, no mileage restriction
Covers cabotage navigation in Chilean waters without distance-to-coast restrictions. Requires deeper knowledge of navigation, meteorology, and regulations. Needed for extensive passage-making through the southern channels.
Level 4
Capitán de Yate
Offshore passage-making, no geographic restriction
The top licence for recreational vessels. Covers offshore routes anywhere in the world. Requires a minimum of 2,500 logged nautical miles and a demanding theoretical and practical assessment.

Which one do I need? For the vast majority of cruising sailors operating in Chile — including the canals region — the Patrón de Yate is the standard choice. The PNN becomes relevant for extensive passage-making through the southern channels or for larger vessels. The Capitán de Yate is for those doing international ocean passages.

Requirements for the Patrón de Yate

Requirements vary slightly between levels, but for the Patrón de Yate — the most common — they are:

  • Minimum age: 18 years old
  • Medical certificate: issued by a doctor approved by Directemar. Evaluates vision, hearing, general physical condition and psychomotor fitness. Not just any doctor — the list of approved doctors is on Directemar's website.
  • Basic First Aid Certificate: from a Directemar-recognised course. Typical duration: 8 hours. Offered by several sailing academies and the Red Cross.
  • Pass the Directemar written exam: covers collision regulations (COLREGS), nautical marks, basic meteorology, VHF/DSC communications, safety, coastal charts and navigation.
  • Pass the practical exam: basic sail and motor manoeuvres, deck safety, anchoring, VHF radio use, distress signals. Conducted at the nearest Capitanía de Puerto.
  • No disqualifying entries in Directemar's Matriculation Register.

The doctor must be Directemar-approved. A certificate from your GP is not valid. Directemar maintains an updated list of approved doctors on their website (gente.directemar.cl). If you submit a certificate from a non-approved doctor, your application will be rejected and you'll need to redo it.

The process step by step

  1. 01
    Enrol in a preparation course
    Technically optional — you can sit the Directemar exam without a course — but virtually nobody does. Sailing club and academy courses cover exactly what the Directemar exam tests and give you access to on-board practice for the practical assessment. Typical duration: 2 to 4 weekends.
  2. 02
    Get the medical certificate
    Book an appointment with a Directemar-approved doctor. Bring your identity document or passport. The certificate is valid for 6 months — don't get it too far in advance of your exam date.
  3. 03
    Complete the First Aid course
    The basic maritime first aid course (8 hours in person) gives you the certificate you'll include in your application dossier. Many academies offer this bundled with the skipper course.
  4. 04
    Submit your dossier to Directemar
    Present at the nearest Capitanía de Puerto: completed application form (Directemar format), copy of identity document or passport, medical certificate, first aid certificate, and the registration fee (a nominal amount). Directemar will assign dates for your written and practical exams.
  5. 05
    Sit the written exam
    Written exam at the Capitanía de Puerto. Covers collision regulations, maritime signals, VHF/DSC communications, basic meteorology, safety, and coastal navigation. Minimum passing score is typically 60%. If you fail, you can resit at the next sitting.
  6. 06
    Sit the practical exam
    On a vessel assigned by Directemar (usually a launch or sailboat at the Capitanía). You'll be asked to demonstrate: port entry and departure, man-overboard manoeuvre, anchoring, VHF use and distress signals, engine handling. If you're an experienced sailor, this is the straightforward part.
  7. 07
    Receive your licence
    With both exams passed, Directemar issues the licence in physical and digital form. Processing time is 1–4 weeks depending on the Capitanía. The licence itself has no fixed expiry, but the medical certificate requires periodic renewal.

Where to study in Chile

Preparation courses for skipper licences are offered mainly by:

  • Sailing clubs — Club de Yates de Valparaíso (CYVSA), Club Náutico de Concepción and Club de Yates Los Colonos (Puerto Montt) all run courses for members and the public during the season. The advantage is on-water access for practice.
  • Private nautical academies — Academies operate in Santiago (yes, landlocked, but with coastal Capitanía arrangements for practice), Valparaíso, Concepción, Puerto Montt and Punta Arenas. Look for those recognised by Directemar.
  • Online + in-person hybrid — Several academies offer online theory and concentrate on-water practice into one or two weekends. Convenient for those living far from the coast.

Tip: Before enrolling, confirm the academy is Directemar-recognised and that their programme covers exactly what the official exam tests. Some courses are excellent on seamanship but light on regulatory content (COLREGS, signals) — which weighs heavily in the written exam. Ask for their first-attempt pass rate at Directemar.

Foreign licences in Chile

If you hold a foreign nautical licence and want to sail in Chile, the process depends on your situation:

  • Short-stay tourists: Directemar generally accepts valid foreign recreational licences during the tourist visa period. Present your original licence (translated into Spanish if in another language) at the Capitanía de Puerto along with your passport and vessel documents. You'll receive a temporary navigation authorisation.
  • Residents or long-stay sailors: If you have Chilean residency or plan to sail regularly over extended seasons, Directemar may require you to sit the Chilean exam or formally demonstrate equivalence. Check directly with the Capitanía in your operating area.
  • RYA licences: International RYA licences are recognised in many countries, but Chile is not on the automatic recognition list. Recognition is at the Capitanía's discretion.
