Required Safety Equipment on Boats in Chile

What Directemar requires by sailing zone — and what's most often missing when they inspect.

By Captain Rodrigo Troncoso  ·  July 2026  ·  13 min read

One of the things that strikes me most when I review boats in Chile is how many sail without complete safety equipment — not from deliberate negligence, but because the exact requirements depend on the sailing zone and available information online mixes different countries and contexts.

This guide covers what Directemar requires in Chile for recreational navigation, broken down by zone. Requirements reflect regulations current at time of writing — always verify with your local Capitanía de Puerto, as Directemar may update its tables.

Chile's sailing zones

Directemar divides navigation into three main categories that determine minimum required equipment:

Zone 1
Restricted Navigation
Inland waters and coves within 3 nm of the coast
Lakes, rivers, inland channels and port manoeuvring areas. Minimum requirements — applies to recreational craft on lakes and rivers.
Zone 2
Coastal Navigation
Up to 12 nautical miles from the coast
The zone for most sailboats and motorboats navigating the Chilean Pacific between coastal ports. Intermediate requirements.
Zone 3 · Highest requirements
Offshore Navigation
More than 12 nautical miles from the coast
Ocean passages, offshore sailing, southern channels in exposed zones. Full survival equipment required.

The southern channels have their own nuance. Although many sections of the Magallanes channels are sheltered and within 12 nm of the coast, Directemar may classify the entire passage as offshore for equipment purposes in the Zarpe — especially if the route crosses exposed zones like the Golfo de Penas or areas without shelter for more than 12 nm.

Expired flares don't meet the requirement. They carry a printed expiry date (typically 3 years from manufacture). An expired flare may be on board, but doesn't count toward the regulatory requirement in an inspection — and you can't rely on it working when you need it most.

Key equipment: what to check on each item

Life jackets
1 per crew + min 1 extra · Marine-certified only
Kayak or freshwater vests without marine certification don't qualify. For offshore: 150N or 275N with integrated harness, location light and retroreflective tape. On auto-inflation models, check the CO₂ cylinder expiry and salt pill condition.
EPIRB
Class 1 (float-free) for offshore · Registered with MRCC Chile
The EPIRB must be registered with up-to-date vessel and owner data in the Cospas-Sarsat/MRCC Chile system. Without registration the signal reaches SAR but there's no vessel to search for. Registration is free on the Chilean Navy site. Battery has an expiry date — check every 2 years.
Liferaft
SOLAS or ISO 9650 · Capacity = total crew · Annual service
The raft must be in an easily accessible cradle or deck-mounted canister. Not locked in a deep cockpit locker that can't be opened in an emergency. Annual service at a certified facility — the service sticker is what gets checked during inspection.
VHF with DSC
Channel 16 mandatory · MMSI registered with Directemar
The VHF's MMSI must be registered with Directemar (simple, free). Without a registered MMSI, DSC distress calls arrive but rescue services can't identify the vessel. A handheld VHF backup is strongly recommended for offshore, with its own charged batteries.
Fire extinguisher
BC (dry powder) or ABC type · Annual inspection
Minimum 1 kg for small vessels; 2 kg+ for boats over 10 m. Must be accessible from the cockpit — there's no point in it being inside a closed compartment under smoke. A car first-aid kit extinguisher doesn't qualify: must be marine or marine-approved.
Marine first aid kit
SOLAS-level or Directemar equivalent contents
Goes well beyond a household kit. For offshore: suturing materials, antibiotics, strong analgesics, IV saline solution (for very remote voyages), compression bandages, tourniquet and a ship's medical handbook. Check expiry dates on every item.

What fails most often in inspections

After participating in surveys and equipment consultations on dozens of vessels in Chile, these are the most common problems I encounter:

  • Expired flares: the most frequent error. Many owners don't track the expiry date and leave them aboard without replacing them.
  • Unregistered EPIRB: the equipment is aboard and functional, but the Cospas-Sarsat registration was never completed. Useless in a real emergency.
  • Liferaft with no current service: the raft exists, but the service sticker is 2–3 years overdue. Directemar doesn't accept rafts without a current annual service.
  • Wrong life jacket type: inflatable jackets with expired CO₂ cylinders, salt pills damaged by humidity, or kayak vests without marine certification.
  • VHF without registered MMSI: the radio is installed and working, but the MMSI was never registered. Channel 16 works, but DSC calls don't identify the vessel.
  • Extinguisher expired or inaccessible: either charge is low but hasn't been refilled, or it's stored where it's impossible to reach under smoke.
