Boat Insurance in Chile: A Practical Guide

What your policy actually covers, what it costs, and how to choose among the insurers operating in Chile.

By Captain Rodrigo Troncoso  ·  July 2026  ·  11 min read

Marine insurance in Chile is one of those topics where online information mixes other countries' rules with local realities in unhelpful ways. Coverages, insurers, and requirements are specific to the Chilean market, and there are important differences between what applies to a motorboat on Lago Villarrica and a cruising sailboat in the southern channels.

This guide is based on what I've seen boat owners in Chile buy and claim over the years. I'm not an insurance broker — for quotes you'll need to go to the insurers directly — but I can explain how the system works and what to look for before you sign.

Is insurance mandatory in Chile?

Short answer: for recreational vessels, not by general law. Unlike car insurance, there is no Chilean law requiring private owners of sailboats or leisure motorboats to insure. The exception is commercial vessels — passenger transport, commercial fishing — where Directemar requires coverage as a condition of operation.

In practice, though, there are situations where you'll need insurance even if it's not legally required:

  • Marina or yacht club: the vast majority require third-party liability insurance when you sign a berth contract. No insurance, no berth.
  • Regattas and rallies: event organizers (Rally del Estrecho, federated regattas) require liability insurance as a condition of entry.
  • Bank financing: if you bought the boat with a loan, the bank typically requires hull insurance on the asset.
  • Charter: if you operate the boat as a charter (even informally), you enter commercial activity and recreational policies no longer apply.

Types of marine insurance

Hull & Machinery (H&M)
Covers physical damage to the vessel — collision, sinking, fire, grounding, total theft of the boat. This is the primary vessel insurance. The sum insured is the agreed value of the boat.
Third-Party Liability (P&I)
Covers damage your vessel causes to third parties — another boat, a marina, a person. This is what marinas and regattas require. Can be taken out separately, without hull cover.
Personal Accident
Covers crew injury or death aboard. Complements personal health insurance but is specific to nautical activity. Important on offshore passages or extended voyages.

The most comprehensive policies combine all three. For regular recreational sailing, the most common combination in Chile is Hull + Liability. Personal accident cover is frequently added for offshore passages or when crew members don't have their own health insurance.

What's covered and what isn't

This is the most important section — and where I've seen the most unpleasant surprises. Nominal coverage can sound broad, but the exclusions are what determine when the insurer will actually pay.

EventCovered?Notes
Collision with another vesselYESLiability covers damage to third parties; hull covers your own damage
Sinking / total lossYESWithin the policy's authorized geographic area
Fire or explosionYESGenerally included in basic hull cover
Theft of the entire vesselDEPENDSRequires proof of force; may have limitations in unmonitored areas
Theft of on-board equipmentNOGenerally excluded or requires a specific rider
Accidental grounding damageDEPENDSAccidental grounding usually yes; negligent grounding may be excluded
Osmosis / fiberglass damageNOExcluded as pre-existing condition and wear
Mechanical engine failureNONormal wear; not a mechanical warranty
Damage during haulout / boatyardDEPENDSRequires explicit shore/land coverage in the policy
Damage outside authorized zoneNOGeographic area is a strict coverage limit
Storm or heavy weather damageYESProvided it's not negligence in foreseeable conditions

The geographic area is critical. If your policy covers "coastal zone up to 200 miles" and you sail into the southern channels or across to Argentina, you are outside coverage. Before any significant passage, review your policy's geographic zone and whether you need an extension.

Insurers operating in Chile

The Chilean marine market is not large, and not every insurer has products well-suited to cruising sailboats. These are the main active options at the time of writing:

  • HDI Seguros: One of the most active in the recreational marine segment. They have experience with cruising sailboats and understand the Chilean market's specifics, including navigation in channels and southern zones. Offer hull, liability and personal accident.
  • Mapfre: Also with recreational sailing experience. Their marine product is well-known in the yacht club circuit. A good option to compare against HDI before deciding.
  • BCI Seguros: Present in the segment, with products more oriented toward lake motorboats and powerboating. Less offshore sailboat experience.
  • Penta Security: Active in the Chilean marine market. Worth quoting for comparison, especially in the lower-value segment.
  • Mutual de Seguros de Chile: Non-profit insurer with history in maritime sectors. Their presence in recreational marine is smaller but exists.
  • Pantaenius / Allianz Marine (international): For high-value ocean-going sailboats or extensive offshore passages, some Chilean owners use international marine specialists. Contracts are direct and in foreign currency. Pantaenius has extensive experience with worldwide passage-making sailboats.

When getting quotes: Don't compare only on price. Compare coverage, authorized geographic area, deductible, and — most importantly — the insurer's experience settling marine claims. An insurer that settles quickly and without complications is worth a price premium.

Always ask for the full policy document (not just the summary sheet) and read the exclusions before signing.

Indicative costs

Premiums depend on multiple factors: insured value, sailing area, skipper's experience, boat use, and the insurer. These are general ranges for recreational sailing in Chile:

Insurance typeEstimated annual costNotes
Liability only (P&I)USD 200 – 800 / yearDepending on coverage limit. Sufficient for marina and regatta requirements
Hull + Liability (basic)1% – 1.5% of value / yearE.g.: USD 60,000 boat → USD 600–900/year
Hull + Liability (offshore, channels)1.5% – 2.5% of value / yearLarger geographic area = higher premium
Full cover (hull + liability + personal accident)2% – 3% of value / yearFor extended passages or crew without their own health insurance

The insured value can be an agreed value (a fixed amount both you and the insurer accept for a total loss) or market value (the insurer determines value at the time of the claim). For sailboats, always prefer agreed value — arguing the market value of a vintage bluewater boat at claim time is a headache you don't need.

What to check before signing

  • Geographic coverage area: Does it include the southern channels, Argentina, international zones? Does it permit crossings to Peruvian or Argentine ports?
  • Insured value and type: Agreed value or market value? For total losses, the difference is enormous.
  • Deductible: How much comes out of your pocket per claim? High deductibles lower the premium but may make it unviable to claim for minor damage.
  • Skipper requirements: Some policies require a minimum licence for coverage to be valid. If the skipper lacks the required title, the insurer can void the claim.
  • Shore coverage: Does it cover the boat in the boatyard or in a crane? Accidents during haulout and yard manoeuvres happen more often than people expect.
  • Claims process: How do you report a claim? How long does settlement take? Ask for recent cases they've handled with similar vessels.
