Registering a Boat with Directemar Chile

Who must register, what documents Directemar asks for and which certificates you need, per the official sources.

By Captain Rodrigo Troncoso  ·  Updated October 2026  ·  8 min read

Before sailing a recreational vessel in Chile, it helps to know what the Maritime Authority requires. The main procedure is the registration (matrícula) of the vessel in the minor-vessel registry; on top of that come the seaworthiness certificate and, depending on the type of passage, departure information. This guide summarises what Directemar's official sources and the General Regulation on Nautical Sports say. Requirements can change, so always confirm with your Capitanía de Puerto.

What is Directemar? The General Directorate of the Maritime Territory and Merchant Marine is the Chilean Navy body that regulates and oversees recreational boating through the General Regulation on Nautical Sports (D.S. (M) No. 214 of 2015).

1. Registering the vessel

Under the Regulation (art. 29), recreational vessels of 50 gross registered tons (GRT) or less are entered in the Minor Vessels Registry run by the Port Captains; those above 50 GRT go in the major-vessels registry of the Directorate General. Once registered, the vessel receives a Registration Certificate showing its name, registration number, holder, tonnage and main characteristics, and it must carry its registration number marked in a visible place (art. 30).

Which vessels must be registered?

Directemar's FAQ states that vessels with an engine under 10 HP, or non-motorised vessels under 5 metres, do not need registration. Above 10 HP (including jet skis), or non-motorised vessels over 5 metres, registration is required. The licences and registration page also lists as exempt, given their activity, size and design, craft such as inflatable dinghies, windsurfers, lasers, optimists, vagabundos and canoes. If you are near a limit, ask the Capitanía.

Documents (vessel bought in Chile)

For the procedure
  • Application for Registration of Minor Recreational Vessels
  • Photocopy of both sides of a valid national ID card
For the vessel
  • Title documents (arts. 831 and 832 of the Commercial Code): a notarised sale contract stating make, model, year, dimensions and engine data (make, model, HP and serial number)
  • Four photographs (bow, stern and both sides) showing the name and the registration number assigned by the Capitanía
  • Plans of the vessel with dimensions and, if applicable, a sail certificate (per Directemar's licences and registration page)

The official pages do not fully agree. The procedure sheet asks only for the application and the vessel's title documents, while the FAQ and the licences and registration page add the ID card, photographs, plans and sail certificate. Bring all of the above and confirm with your Capitanía what they require.

Vessel bought abroad

Directemar asks, besides the application, for: the vessel title (sale contract signed before a notary or equivalent in the country of sale, legalised under art. 345 of the Code of Civil Procedure), the deregistration certificate from the country of origin (also legalised) and the customs import documents (import declaration, assessment of duties and proof of payment).

Process, timing and cost

  1. Gather your documents — As listed above, and confirm the current list with your Capitanía.
  2. Submit the application at a Capitanía de Puerto — The procedure is carried out at the relevant Capitanía.
  3. Pay the fees — Amounts are in Directemar's Schedule of Fees and Tariffs, calculated at the day's US dollar value and paid in Chilean pesos at the same Capitanía, at Banco BCI branches or Caja Vecina BancoEstado.
  4. Receive your certificate — Directemar states 48 working hours after documents and payment are submitted. The result is the Registration Certificate and the vessel's Safety Certificate.

I do not quote peso amounts here because fees depend on the Schedule of Fees and the dollar rate. Check the current value in the official publication (in Spanish) or at your Capitanía.

2. Seaworthiness certificate and equipment

According to Directemar, registered recreational vessels must hold a Certificate of Seaworthiness attesting to their navigability and equipment. It is valid for up to 6 years, unless there is a structural change or major repair, in which case it must be renewed.

The Regulation (art. 31) requires vessels to carry the safety, radio-communication and navigation equipment needed, with current certifications from the Maritime Authority. The detail by vessel type is set by Circular A-41/014 (in Spanish); for a practical review of your gear, see the safety equipment guide.

3. Call sign and MMSI

Directemar offers an online service to request the call sign and ISMM (MMSI) for a minor vessel. If your boat has a DSC VHF, request that number through the official platform and confirm with your Capitanía whether it is required in your case.

4. Zarpe and crew registration

The Regulation (art. 34) states that Chilean recreational vessels sailing in Chilean jurisdictional waters do not require zarpe authorisation, but the skipper must inform the club or boating entity they depart from or, if none exists, the local Maritime Authority, of their intended movement. Directemar also runs the online INTERMAR system to register crew, passengers, zarpes and port calls of minor recreational vessels. Details are in the zarpe guide.

Foreign vessels in Chile

Foreign recreational vessels must carry documents proving their right to fly their country's flag (art. 30), and their crews must request written authorisation to sail Chilean inland waters at the first port of arrival, follow the rules the Maritime Authority sets and carry charts of the sailing area (art. 36). If you plan to stay in Chile with a boat bought abroad, the route is customs import and the registration described above.

Transfer and cancellation

To sell, transfer or deregister a registered vessel, title is shown by a notarised sale contract (arts. 831 and 832 of the Commercial Code). The exact steps for transfer and cancellation are defined by each Capitanía, so check there before signing.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to register my boat even if it is small?
According to Directemar, vessels with an engine under 10 HP and non-motorised vessels under 5 metres do not need registration. Above those limits, including jet skis, registration is mandatory. If in doubt, ask your Capitanía de Puerto.
How long does registration take?
Directemar states 48 working hours after the documents are submitted and the fees paid. If your case involves customs import or other documents, the whole process may take longer.
Do I need a zarpe to go sailing?
The Regulation states that Chilean recreational vessels do not need zarpe authorisation in Chilean waters, but the skipper must inform the club or the local Maritime Authority of their intended movement. See the zarpe guide and confirm the procedure at your Capitanía.
Can I register a boat bought abroad in Chile?
Yes. Besides the application, Directemar asks for the legalised vessel title, the legalised deregistration certificate from the country of origin and the customs import documents (import declaration, assessment of duties and proof of payment).
