Why it matters

Buying well is the first nautical safety measure

A vessel in poor condition is not just an expensive headache: it can be a safety hazard. Structural problems, a failing engine or a deficient electrical system can compromise the safety of the crew at the worst possible moment.

Most buyers don't have the technical eye to detect these problems in a listing or even in a visual inspection. That's what this advisory is for: to give you the professional perspective before you spend a single peso.

Captain Rodrigo has inspected and advised on the purchase of dozens of vessels in Chile — from coastal sailboats to offshore work boats. He knows what to look for and, above all, what sellers prefer not to show.

What's covered

What this advisory reviews

  • Listing and vessel characteristics review
  • Risk detection from photos and documentation
  • Key questions to ask the seller
  • Engine condition and maintenance history
  • Rig state (for sailboats): mast, shrouds, sails
  • Electrical system assessment
  • Operating cost guidance
  • Navy documentation check
  • Pre-purchase sea trial advice
  • Overall buy/don't-buy recommendation
FAQ

Questions I'm often asked

What does the used vessel purchase advisory include?
Review of the listing and vessel characteristics, detection of visible risks in photos and documentation, a list of key questions to ask the seller, and guidance on maintenance costs, expected use and safety. All online before you go see it or pay anything.
Can I get advisory if the sailboat is in another city?
Yes. The advisory is fully online: send me the listing, photos and available information, and I'll give you my assessment by WhatsApp or video call. I don't need to be physically present for this initial review.
What mistakes do used vessel buyers most commonly make?
The most common: buying without inspecting the hull out of water, not asking about the engine's maintenance history, not verifying the rig condition on a sailboat, underestimating repair and maintenance costs, and not checking documentation with Directemar.
Is a physical inspection needed in addition to the online advisory?
For higher-value vessels, I do recommend complementing the online advisory with a physical inspection by a marine surveyor at the boat's location. The online advisory helps you filter and discard options before spending time and money on physical visits.
